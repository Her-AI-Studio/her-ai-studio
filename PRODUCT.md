# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

High school girls, grades 9–12, who are curious about AI but aren't sure they belong in tech. They've heard "AI" in school or online, feel excited but intimidated, and need reassurance before they'll commit. Parents and educators may also arrive at this page, but the primary design target is the girl herself.

## Product Purpose

Her AI Studio is taking shape as a free, cohort-based program for girls to learn to create with AI (chatbots, art generators, apps) in a community of peers and mentors who look like them. **Right now the site collects interest** via a Google Form so we can notify people when dates, details, and applications are ready. Success looks like a curious girl or a supportive adult joining the list without friction.

## Brand Personality

Hands-on, unpretentious, technically confident. Her AI Studio is a workshop, not a polished EdTech pitch — a place that proves itself with real builds and real code rather than marketing gloss. Voice: direct, matter-of-fact, a little irreverent about failure ("half of these caught fire" is a feature of the story, not something to hide). Three words: authentic, technical, unglossy.

Pivoted 2026-08-29 from a warmer "expressive, welcoming, electric" sisterhood framing toward this grittier materials-lab register, modeled after the `ai-forge-femme` reference direction. The underlying mission and audience are unchanged; the surface voice and aesthetic are not.

## Anti-references

- Shiny, glossy EdTech marketing sites that oversell and read like a sales pitch rather than an honest workshop
- Generic diverse-teens-at-laptops stock photography
- Corporate tech landing pages (hero metric templates, enterprise tone)
- Overly "girly" pink-and-pastel designs that condescend to the audience
- Generic SaaS sites (card grids, stat sections, testimonial carousels)
- Terminal-black/dark-mode "hacker" aesthetic — the workshop is bright and paper-lit, not a dark room. Monospace is welcome, but only as an accent inside real code blocks, never as the page's primary voice.

## Design Principles

1. **Technical authorship** — She owns the thing she built, not just "was inspired by" it. Copy says "her build," never "a project she helped with."
2. **Honest about failure** — Building includes breaking things. Don't sanitize the messy parts; confidence comes from proof, not polish.
3. **A materials problem, not a recruitment problem** — Frame the mission around access to real hardware, tools, and mentorship, not "getting more girls interested."
4. **Peer and near-peer mentorship, radical transparency** — College-age mentors, not distant experts. Explain how the tech and the program actually work; don't gatekeep the internals.
5. **Concrete over abstract** — Real names, real project titles, real parts lists. Not "students" but "Luna, grade 9." Never fabricate finished student work for a program that hasn't run its first cohort yet — show real prototype builds and mentor work, honestly framed as "here's the kind of thing you'll make."

## Accessibility and Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced-motion respected for scroll reveals and decorative motion. All form fields labeled. The workshop aesthetic stays light-mode (paper background, ink-charcoal text); check contrast wherever the utilitarian accent color sits on the paper background or vice versa.
